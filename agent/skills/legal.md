# Legal playbook

## When this becomes relevant

- Collecting any user data, PII, or payment information.
- Going live, public, or taking paying customers.
- Hiring, contracting, or working with partners.
- Choosing a business entity, equity split, or IP ownership.
- Handling content, trademarks, or copyright.
- Operating in regulated industries (health, finance, education, GDPR, COPPA).

## Questions to ask

- What data do you collect, where is it stored, and where are your users located?
- Do you have a privacy policy and terms of service that match what you actually do?
- Are you processing payments, health, financial, or child data?
- Do users or third parties create content you host or distribute?
- Are you using trademarks, open source code, or third-party assets?
- Are founders, employees, or contractors assigned IP and bound by confidentiality?

## Common anti-patterns

- Copying a privacy policy or terms from another site without review.
- Launching without either document because "we are too small."
- Storing more data than you need or keeping it forever.
- Letting founders/contractors build IP without a written agreement.
- Using a name or logo without a trademark search.
- Ignoring regional laws (GDPR, CCPA, PIPEDA) because you are not based there.

## Decision heuristics

- Add a privacy policy and terms before you take real users or payments.
- Collect the minimum data you need and delete it when done.
- Use plain language in legal pages; do not hide important terms.
- Separate user content ownership clearly in your terms.
- Get IP assignment and confidentiality in writing before someone writes code.
- When in doubt, pay a lawyer once for the core documents and templates.

## Teaching moments

- "Do I need a privacy policy?" → If you collect data, yes, and it must match your real practices.
- "Can I copy terms from Stripe?" → Only as a starting point; your product, jurisdiction, and data use differ.
- "When should I incorporate?" → Before taking money, signing contracts, or hiring.
- "What if a user in Europe signs up?" → GDPR basics, consent, right to deletion, and data processing records.
