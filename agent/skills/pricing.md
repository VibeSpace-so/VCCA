# Pricing playbook

## When this becomes relevant

- Choosing a business model (SaaS, usage, seat, transactional).
- Setting or changing prices.
- Deciding between free tier, free trial, or paid-only.
- Adding enterprise or annual plans.
- Packaging features into tiers.

## Questions to ask

- Who pays, and who makes the buying decision?
- What value metric best maps to customer success?
- What is the customer already spending to solve this problem?
- Are we pricing for value delivered or our cost to serve?
- What is the customer's willingness to pay?
- How does price affect acquisition and retention?
- Can a customer understand the pricing in 10 seconds?

## Common anti-patterns

- "Free forever" without a conversion plan.
- Under-pricing to win early users.
- Copying an incumbent's pricing without validating it.
- No pricing experiments or annual option.
- Charging by seat when the value is usage or outcome.
- Complex tiers with more than three options.

## Decision heuristics

- Price based on the value you deliver, not what you cost.
- Start higher than you think; it is easier to discount than to raise.
- Offer an annual plan with a meaningful discount.
- Keep tiers simple: free trial or free tier, plus two paid tiers.
- Measure payback period, LTV, and gross margin by segment.
- Separate pricing experiments from product experiments.

## Teaching moments

- "How much should I charge?" → Willingness-to-pay interviews and Van Westendorp.
- "Freemium or free trial?" → Free trial converts better for B2B; freemium works for viral bottoms-up.
- "Should I have three tiers?" → Anchor, good-better-best, or good-better-best-plus-enterprise.
