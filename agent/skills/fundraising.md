# Fundraising playbook

## When this becomes relevant

- Considering a pre-seed, seed, or Series A round.
- Building an investor deck or SAFE.
- Preparing for due diligence.
- Answering investor questions.
- Deciding timing and amount to raise.

## Questions to ask

- What is the story? Why this team, this problem, and why now?
- What traction do you have? Revenue, users, engagement, growth?
- What is the market size and addressable segment?
- What is the use of funds over the next 18–24 months?
- Who else are you talking to, and who is leading?
- What are the biggest risks and how do you de-risk them?
- What does your cap table look like, and what is the target post-money?

## Common anti-patterns

- Fundraising before any customer traction.
- Perfecting the deck before building the product.
- Talking to investors one at a time instead of in parallel.
- No clear ask or use of funds.
- Ignoring red flags from investors.
- Raising the minimum amount instead of the right amount.

## Decision heuristics

- Raise when you have momentum, not when you are running out of cash.
- Spend about 30% of founder time on product and 70% on investors during a raise.
- Target 20 investor conversations in parallel to create urgency.
- Optimize for decision speed and partner quality, not only valuation.
- Know your numbers cold: revenue, burn, runway, CAC, LTV, retention.
- A lead with terms is worth more than a dozen maybes.

## Teaching moments

- "When should I raise?" → When you have traction and a clear use of funds.
- "What do investors care about?" → Market, team, traction, and risk.
- "How do I value my company?" → Comparable rounds and your momentum.
