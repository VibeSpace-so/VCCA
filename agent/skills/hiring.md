# Hiring playbook

## When this becomes relevant

- The founder is doing everything and needs help.
- Hiring first engineers, designers, sales, or operators.
- Deciding between contractors, full-time, and equity.
- Writing job descriptions or interview plans.
- Onboarding the first employees.
- Defining culture, values, or ways of working.

## Questions to ask

- What is the one thing you need most that you cannot do yourself?
- Is this a full-time need or a short-term project?
- What would a great hire accomplish in their first 90 days?
- What values and behaviors matter most on this team?
- How much can you pay in cash? What equity can you offer?
- Do you have repeatable work and clear expectations for them?

## Common anti-patterns

- Hiring generalists for a specific, urgent problem.
- Copying job descriptions from big tech companies.
- Hiring too fast because you are desperate, then firing too slow.
- Promising equity without a clear vesting schedule or cliff.
- Onboarding by throwing someone into the deep end with no context.
- Hiring friends or family without the same rigor as strangers.
- Building a team before validating the product direction.

## Decision heuristics

- Hire for the next 6–12 months of pain, not a 5-year vision.
- Optimize for slope (rate of improvement) over intercept (current skill).
- Senior generalists are usually better for early stage than narrow specialists.
- Every hire should solve a real, measured bottleneck.
- Be explicit about equity, vesting, and expectations in writing.
- Invest in onboarding; a confused hire loses months of productivity.

## Teaching moments

- "When should I hire my first engineer?" → When the product direction is clear and you are the bottleneck.
- "How much equity should I give?" → Depends on role, stage, and cash; use benchmarks but stay consistent.
- "Contractor or employee?" → Contractors for scoped projects; employees for ongoing ownership.
- "How do I interview early hires?" → Work-sample project, then reference checks, then culture fit.
