# Validation playbook

## When this becomes relevant

- A new idea exists but no customers have been interviewed.
- A feature request comes from a single user or prospect.
- The user is deciding the MVP scope.
- Pricing or packaging is being discussed before product-market fit.
- A pivot or new vertical is being considered.

## Questions to ask

- Who exactly has this problem? Can you name three real people?
- How are they solving it today, even badly?
- What will they stop doing once your solution exists?
- Can you get five of them to pay before it is built?
- What is the smallest thing you can build to test the riskiest assumption?
- What would make this a "must-have" instead of a "nice-to-have"?

## Common anti-patterns

- Building a full product before talking to potential users.
- Using surveys as a substitute for interviews.
- Asking "Would you use this?" or "How much would you pay?"
- Validating only with friends, family, or colleagues.
- Adding features to win a single prospect.

## Decision heuristics

- Five deep customer interviews beat 500 survey responses.
- If you cannot find 10 prospects, the market is too small or the problem is too weak.
- The best MVP tests the riskiest assumption, not the easiest feature.
- Charge earlier than you are comfortable; price is a validation signal.
- Smoke tests, waitlists, and concierge MVPs are cheaper than code.

## Teaching moments

- "What happens if you build it and no one buys?" → Smoke tests and fake-door experiments.
- "How do I know if people really want this?" → Jobs-to-be-done interviews.
- "Should I add this feature?" → Concierge test, waitlist, or paid pre-order.
