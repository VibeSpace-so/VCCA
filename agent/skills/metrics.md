# Metrics playbook

## When this becomes relevant

- Defining what success looks like for a feature or the whole product.
- Building a dashboard, analytics pipeline, or event tracking.
- Running experiments, A/B tests, or cohort analysis.
- Preparing for growth, fundraising, or investor updates.
- Understanding retention, activation, churn, or revenue.
- Choosing between vanity metrics and actionable metrics.

## Questions to ask

- What is the one metric that, if it improves, tells you the product is working?
- How do users get value? What is the "aha" moment or activation event?
- How often do users come back? What makes them stop?
- What does it cost to acquire a user? What is their lifetime value?
- What is the conversion funnel from visitor to paying customer?
- Are you measuring outcomes (revenue, retention) or outputs (features shipped)?

## Common anti-patterns

- Tracking too many metrics and acting on none.
- Optimizing for signups instead of activation or retention.
- Using cumulative charts that always go up.
- Running A/B tests without a hypothesis or enough sample size.
- Measuring team productivity by lines of code or story points.
- Ignoring qualitative feedback because "the data says otherwise."

## Decision heuristics

- Start with one north-star metric and a small set of inputs that drive it.
- Prefer cohort-based retention over cumulative user counts.
- Track the full funnel, not just the top.
- Measure retention before worrying about acquisition scale.
- Run experiments to reduce risk, not to prove you are right.
- Pair every quantitative insight with at least five customer conversations.

## Teaching moments

- "What is a north-star metric?" → The single metric that captures the core value users get.
- "What is activation?" → The moment a new user experiences the product's value.
- "What is CAC vs LTV?" → Cost to acquire a customer vs the revenue they generate over time.
- "What is a cohort?" → A group of users who started in the same period, tracked together.
